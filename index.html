1. Blood Count & Clotting

Test What It Assesses Why Essential at 70+ Symptoms / Red Flags if Abnormal Key Notes / Instructions
CBC with Differential Red cells, white cells, platelets, hemoglobin, hematocrit Detects anemia, infections, blood disorders, and leukemia; fatigue from anemia is often dismissed as "just old age" in India Fatigue, weakness, pallor, frequent infections, easy bruising, shortness of breath No fasting. Ask specifically for "CBC with differential," not just hemoglobin. Also ask for RBC indices (MCV/RDW) — high MCV → B12/folate deficiency; low MCV → iron deficiency. Iron-deficiency anemia in an elderly male = colon cancer until proven otherwise; insist on a cause workup, don't just give iron. Abnormal results may require a blood smear follow-up.
ESR Non-specific inflammation marker Screens for hidden infections, autoimmune disease, or cancer Persistent low-grade fever, unexplained weight loss, joint stiffness Non-specific; if elevated, doctor will order follow-up tests to find the cause. Ask for both ESR and hs-CRP, not just one.
Peripheral Blood Smear Shape and size of blood cells Identifies abnormal cells, parasites (e.g., malaria in India), and megaloblastic anemia Same as CBC abnormalities Usually done only if CBC is abnormal — saves unnecessary cost. The lab physically looks at cells under a microscope for abnormalities.
PT / INR & aPTT Blood clotting time Baseline before any procedure; essential if father is on or may need blood thinners Easy bruising, nosebleeds, blood in stool or urine, prolonged bleeding from cuts Tell doctor if he takes any blood-thinning medication or herbal supplements.
Hb Electrophoresis Hemoglobin variants Rules out trait/disease like Thalassemia, which can cause unexplained mild anemia often confused with iron deficiency Mild, persistent fatigue Crucial to do this before starting any iron supplements for anemia — taking iron blindly can be harmful in Thalassemia trait.

2. Diabetes & Metabolic Health

Test What It Assesses Why Essential at 70+ Symptoms / Red Flags if Abnormal Key Notes / Instructions
Fasting Blood Glucose / Fasting Plasma Glucose (FPG) Blood sugar after overnight fast Indians have very high genetic risk for Type 2 diabetes; many cases are silent Excessive thirst, frequent urination (especially at night), fatigue, blurred vision, slow-healing wounds, unexplained weight loss 10–12 hours fasting. Water allowed. Avoid alcohol the night before. Diagnostic cutoff ≥126 mg/dL (ICMR 2018). Needs 8–10 hr fast.
Postprandial Blood Glucose (PPG) Blood sugar 2 hours after a meal Catches diabetes missed by fasting alone; reflects real-world glucose control Same as fasting glucose abnormalities Often included in diabetes monitoring. If diabetic, this is checked alongside fasting glucose.
HbA1c 3-month average blood sugar Gold standard for diagnosing diabetes and measuring control; more reliable than single glucose reading Often completely asymptomatic in early stages No fasting needed. Can be done any time of day. ⚠ Not the sole test in India — anemia and thalassemia trait can distort it. Pair with FPG at an NGSP/IFCC-certified lab.
Fasting Insulin Insulin level in blood Detects insulin resistance before diabetes develops; allows early lifestyle reversal Weight gain around abdomen, fatigue, sugar cravings Fasting required. Often omitted from basic packages — ask specifically.
Urine Microalbumin/Creatinine Ratio (UACR / ACR) Early kidney damage from diabetes/hypertension Diabetes is the #1 cause of kidney failure in India; this catches it when still reversible Foamy urine, swelling in legs/face First-morning urine sample is best. ICMR mandates annual ACR in diabetics. Combine with eGFR for KDIGO staging.

TestWhat It AssessesWhy Essential at 70+Symptoms / Red Flags if AbnormalKey Notes / Instructions
Cystatin C True kidney filtration (no muscle-mass bias) Confirms/refutes borderline creatinine eGFR in sarcopenic elderly; gives far more accurate picture of true kidney health than creatinine alone Reflects true GFR Order alongside creatinine if eGFR borderline (45–75) or if frail. 5. Liver Function

Test What It Assesses Why Essential at 70+ Symptoms / Red Flags if Abnormal Key Notes / Instructions
LFT Panel (AST, ALT, ALP, GGT, Bilirubin, Albumin, Total Protein) Liver cell health, bile flow, protein production Fatty liver (NAFLD) is extremely common in India due to diet and diabetes; detects hepatitis, medication toxicity; baseline before statins Jaundice (yellow eyes/skin), fatigue, abdominal swelling, dark urine, confusion Usually no fasting. Avoid alcohol for at least 3 days prior for accurate GGT. Tell doctor about all regular meds including OTC painkillers. Tie low albumin to nutrition assessment.
Gamma-GT (GGT) Bile duct and liver enzyme Sensitive marker of liver stress and alcohol consumption; often missed in basic panels Same as above Even moderate alcohol or certain medications can raise this.

6. Thyroid Function

Test What It Assesses Why Essential at 70+ Symptoms / Red Flags if Abnormal Key Notes / Instructions
TSH Thyroid regulation Hypothyroidism is very common in elderly Indians; worsens cholesterol, depression, and cognition; often misdiagnosed as "just getting old" Weight gain, cold intolerance, depression, constipation, dry skin, hair loss, slowed thinking Morning test preferred. Stop biotin supplements (hair/skin vitamins) 2 days before — they skew results. ⚠ Use age-adjusted ranges — upper limit ~6–7 mIU/L for >70 (standard 0.4–4.5 over-diagnoses). Don't rush to treat mild elevations; repeat in 2–3 months.
Free T3 & Free T4 Active thyroid hormone levels Confirms diagnosis and severity if TSH is abnormal Same as above Usually done only if TSH is abnormal to control cost. Insist on Free T3 and Free T4 alongside TSH — TSH alone doesn't show how the body is converting active hormone.
Anti-TPO Antibodies Autoimmune thyroiditis (Hashimoto's) Identifies autoimmune cause; very common in Indian population Same as above One-time test if TSH is abnormal.

7. Vitamins, Minerals & Iron

Test What It Assesses Why Essential at 70+ Symptoms / Red Flags if Abnormal Key Notes / Instructions
Vitamin D (25-OH) Vitamin D storage 50–94% of Indians deficient despite sunlight; critical for bones, immunity, and falls prevention Bone pain, muscle weakness, frequent falls, low mood/depression No fasting. Insist on 25-hydroxyvitamin D (not 1,25-dihydroxy). Deficiency 30. Test before supplementing to avoid toxicity.
Vitamin B12 Nerve and blood cell health 42–62% of Indian elderly deficient. Causes reversible dementia, neuropathy, anemia, and falls — one of the most treatable findings Numbness/tingling in hands/feet, memory problems, fatigue, anemia, difficulty walking, glossitis Fasting preferred. ⚠ Routine serum B12 often inaccurate. Ask for Active B12 (Holotranscobalamin) or MMA (methylmalonic acid) if borderline (150–300 pg/mL) — more sensitive.
Folate (Vitamin B9) DNA synthesis and cell division Often co-deficient with B12; both cause macrocytic anemia Same as B12 + sore tongue, mouth ulcers Usually tested alongside B12. Interpret with B12 — treating folate alone while B12 is low can worsen neurological damage.
Iron Studies (Serum Iron, TIBC, Ferritin, Transferrin Saturation) Iron storage and transport Distinguishes true iron deficiency from anemia of chronic disease; iron-deficiency anemia in elderly male mandates GI-bleed/cancer search Fatigue, pallor, brittle nails, craving ice or starch (pica) Morning fasting preferred. Ferritin is an acute-phase reactant — interpret with CRP. Fasting morning ideal.
Serum Calcium (Albumin-Corrected) Calcium level Bone health, nerve signaling, heart rhythm; ties into bone/vitamin D axis Muscle cramps, numbness, irregular heartbeat, osteoporosis Ask for albumin-corrected calcium. If abnormal, add PTH.
Serum Magnesium Magnesium level Frequently deficient and rarely tested; critical for heart rhythm, blood pressure, and bone density Muscle cramps, tremors, irregular heartbeat, anxiety, constipation Often omitted from basic packages — ask specifically.
Serum Phosphorus Phosphorus level Kidney and bone metabolism Weakness, bone pain, itching Usually part of basic metabolic panel.
PTH (Parathyroid Hormone) Parathyroid/calcium disorders Ordered only if calcium or vitamin D abnormal; high → bone loss, fractures, kidney stones Bone pain, fractures, stones, fatigue Pair with corrected calcium + 25(OH)D.
Testosterone (Total, Morning) Male hormone levels Low testosterone is underdiagnosed in elderly men; contributes to fatigue, low mood, muscle loss, osteoporosis Low energy, depression, reduced muscle mass, low libido, cognitive fog, anemia Morning sample (8–10 AM), fasting. Optional/symptom-driven only — not for "anti-ageing." Many medications affect this.

8. Cardiac Investigations

Test What It Assesses Why Essential at 70+ Symptoms / Red Flags if Abnormal Key Notes / Instructions
Blood Pressure — seated, both arms, repeat; home/ambulatory Hypertension #1 modifiable stroke/heart-failure risk in this age group; often completely silent Usually none; occasional headache, dizziness at extremes USPSTF Grade A. Check orthostatic BP (lying + standing at 1 & 3 min) — major reversible fall cause. Consider 24-hr ambulatory BP for white-coat/masked HTN. Get checked at more than one visit.
Resting ECG Heart rhythm, electrical activity, evidence of past silent heart attack Baseline screening; "silent" heart attacks and atrial fibrillation are common in elderly diabetics Palpitations, breathlessness, dizziness, unexplained fatigue Non-invasive; 5 minutes. No prep. Quick, cheap, valuable baseline.
2D Echocardiogram Heart pumping strength (EF%), valve function, chamber size, diastolic function Detects heart failure (especially HFpEF in elderly/hypertensives) and valve disease (aortic stenosis is common in elderly) Breathlessness on walking, leg swelling, fatigue, waking up breathless Ultrasound; no preparation. Ask specifically to assess diastolic function + aortic valve.
TMT / Stress Test (or Stress Echo) Heart performance under physical stress Detects blocked coronary arteries not visible at rest Chest tightness on climbing stairs or walking, breathlessness on exertion ⚠ Only if symptomatic or intermediate risk — NOT a blind screen. Avoid if severe knee/hip arthritis; a pharmacological stress test can be done instead if mobility is limited.
Coronary Artery Calcium (CAC) Score — CT Calcified plaque burden in heart arteries Refines CV risk; 0 = reassuring, high = reclassifies risk upward None by design Extra cost, low radiation. Less discriminating at advanced age (most 74-year-olds have some calcium). Discuss value with cardiologist, especially if family history of heart disease.
Carotid Intima-Media Thickness (CIMT) Thickness of inner carotid artery layers Visualizes arterial plaque; direct window into stroke risk Dizziness, TIA symptoms (temporary vision loss, slurred speech, one-sided numbness) Radiation-free ultrasound add-on if any cardiac risk.
hs-CRP Low-grade artery inflammation Predicts heart attack risk independent of cholesterol; elevated = higher vascular risk Usually asymptomatic No fasting. Will be temporarily elevated by any active infection — inform doctor if he has a cold. Order hs-CRP (cardiac-specific), not regular CRP. Target
Homocysteine Amino acid toxic to blood vessels; B12/folate status Independent risk factor for heart attack, stroke, and dementia; often missed Usually asymptomatic; brain fog, neuropathy, fatigue Fasting preferred; can often be lowered with B-vitamin supplementation. Target
NT-proBNP / BNP Heart failure strain marker Detects early heart failure before obvious symptoms develop Breathlessness, leg swelling, fatigue Usually ordered if there are symptoms or risk factors.

9. Cancer Screenings & Tumor Markers

Test What It Assesses Why Essential at 70+ Symptoms / Red Flags if Abnormal Key Notes / Instructions
PSA — Total & Free/Total Ratio Prostate cancer screening Prostate cancer is one of the most common cancers in Indian men; early detection is curable Frequent urination, weak urine stream, blood in urine, pelvic discomfort ⚠ Controversial — discuss with urologist. Use age-adjusted cutoff (≤6.5 ng/mL for 70s, not 4.0). If total 4–10 (gray zone), order Free/Total ratio — ≤0.10 → ~65% cancer risk; >0.25 → ~16%. Do DRE too. Avoid ejaculation for 48 hours before test; DRE done just before can slightly raise PSA.
Digital Rectal Exam (DRE) Physical prostate examination Detects nodules or hard areas that PSA blood test misses Same as above Uncomfortable but essential; should be done in addition to PSA, not instead of it.
Colonoscopy Direct visualization of colon and rectum Gold standard for colon cancer screening; colorectal cancer rates are rising in India Blood in stool, change in bowel habits, unexplained anemia, abdominal pain Requires 1-day bowel prep (clear liquids + laxative); sedation is used. If normal, typically repeated every 10 years. If never screened and fit for prep, this is probably the single most valuable test on the list.
Fecal Immunochemical Test (FIT) Hidden blood in stool Less invasive screening alternative to colonoscopy Same as above No dietary restrictions. Do annually if colonoscopy is declined or unavailable. A positive result means colonoscopy is mandatory.
Upper GI Endoscopy Stomach/esophagus lining Catches ulcers, H. pylori, early stomach cancer — more common in India than the West Persistent indigestion, trouble swallowing, weight loss Do it same-day as colonoscopy if he's already being sedated — saves a trip.
Oral Cavity Examination Oral/oropharyngeal cancer India = world's highest oral cancer burden (tobacco/betel-nut/gutka/paan) White/red patches, non-healing ulcers (>2 weeks), lumps, swallowing difficulty Visual + palpation by dentist/ENT. High yield if any tobacco/areca-nut use.
Dermatology Skin Exam Full-body mole and lesion check Skin cancers become more common with age; early melanoma is treatable New or changing moles, non-healing sores, irregular borders or color 10–15 minute dermatologist check. Easy to slot in.
CEA Marker for colorectal cancer Used mainly for monitoring after treatment, not for primary screening Same as above Can be elevated in smokers; not a standalone diagnostic test.
Chest X-Ray Lung masses, infections, heart enlargement Baseline check; India has high TB burden Persistent cough, breathlessness, weight loss Low radiation; good baseline. Consider if cough, weight loss, night sweats, diabetes, or pre-procedure baseline.
Low-Dose CT Chest (LDCT) Detailed lung imaging Far more sensitive than X-ray for early lung cancer detection Same as above Recommended for heavy smokers (50–80 years, ≥20 pack-years, current or quit

10. Whole-Body Imaging

Test What It Assesses Why Essential at 70+ Symptoms / Red Flags if Abnormal Key Notes / Instructions
Ultrasound — Whole Abdomen & Pelvis Liver, gallbladder, pancreas, kidneys, spleen, prostate, bladder, aorta Non-invasive way to spot fatty liver, gallstones, kidney stones/cysts, enlarged prostate, abdominal aortic aneurysm Abdominal pain, bloating, urinary difficulty, back pain Fasting 6–8 hours required for upper abdomen imaging. Full bladder needed for prostate view. Specify: include abdominal aorta diameter (AAA screen) + post-void residual urine + prostate volume.
CT Coronary Angiography (CTCA) Calcium and plaque in heart arteries Non-invasive alternative to invasive catheter angiography for selected high-risk patients Chest pain, breathlessness on exertion Requires iodine contrast dye — kidney function (creatinine/eGFR) must be checked first. Not suitable for everyone.
Carotid Doppler Blood flow in neck arteries to brain Detects plaque and narrowing that causes strokes Transient vision loss, dizziness, one-sided weakness or numbness Non-invasive ultrasound; no preparation.
Ankle-Brachial Index (ABI) Blood pressure comparison arm vs. ankle Screens for peripheral artery disease (blocked leg arteries) — common in diabetics and smokers Leg pain when walking that stops with rest, non-healing foot wounds, cold feet Simple, non-invasive; highly underutilized.
Abdominal Aortic Aneurysm (AAA) Ultrasound Aortic aneurysm ⚠ USPSTF Grade B: one-time for men 65–75 who ever smoked (≥100 cigarettes). Silent and lethal if ruptures Usually silent; pulsatile abdominal mass; rupture = catastrophic One-time only. Can be bundled into abdominal ultrasound. Establish smoking history first.

11. Urology & Men's Health

Test What It Assesses Why Essential at 70+ Symptoms / Red Flags if Abnormal Key Notes / Instructions
Urinalysis (Routine & Microscopy) Infection, blood, protein, sugar in urine UTIs in elderly men are often silent but can lead to kidney infection; also screens for kidney disease and diabetes Burning urination, frequency, urgency, cloudy or foul-smelling urine First morning, mid-stream sample is most accurate. Persistent microscopic hematuria in elderly male → urology referral (bladder cancer).
Urine Culture & Sensitivity Specific bacteria causing infection Identifies exact organism and correct antibiotic (critical with rising antibiotic resistance in India) Same as above Takes 48–72 hours. Do this if urinalysis suggests infection.
Serum Testosterone (Total & Free, Morning) Male hormone levels Low testosterone is underdiagnosed in elderly men; causes fatigue, depression, bone loss, and low quality of life Low energy, depression, reduced muscle mass, low libido, cognitive fog Morning sample (8–10 AM) is most accurate. Many medications affect this. Optional/symptom-driven only.
Post-Void Residual (PVR) Urine left in bladder after urinating Detects enlarged prostate obstruction or weak bladder muscle Weak stream, dribbling, feeling of incomplete emptying, frequent night urination Non-invasive bladder scan; often done with urologist.
IPSS Questionnaire + Uroflowmetry Prostate outflow obstruction (BPH) BPH near-universal at this age; affects quality of life, can cause retention/kidney damage Weak stream, frequency, nocturia, incomplete emptying Complete IPSS before urology visit. Note: nocturia may be sleep apnea/diabetes/edema/diuretics, not just prostate.

12. Bone Health

Test What It Assesses Why Essential at 70+ Symptoms / Red Flags if Abnormal Key Notes / Instructions
DEXA Scan (Bone Mineral Density, hip + lumbar spine) Bone density and osteoporosis risk Men also get osteoporosis; a hip fracture in a 70+ man has very high mortality and disability risk Back pain, height loss, stooped posture, fractures from minor falls Low radiation; takes ~15 minutes. Central DEXA (hip + spine), not peripheral. Bring any prior DEXA report for comparison. T-score ≤−2.5 = osteoporosis. If spine reading artifactually high from arthritis, ask about QCT or hip-priority read.
Serum Calcium, Phosphorus, ALP Bone metabolism markers Supports DEXA interpretation and detects metabolic bone disorders Bone pain, fractures, deformities ALP is part of LFT; calcium/phosphorus usually in basic panel.
Vertebral Fracture Assessment (VFA) or Lateral Spine X-ray Silent spinal compression fractures Catches fractures DEXA misses Height loss >3–4 cm, kyphosis, chronic back pain Add if height loss, chronic back pain, steroid use, or low BMD.

13. Neurology & Cognition

Test What It Assesses Why Essential at 70+ Symptoms / Red Flags if Abnormal Key Notes / Instructions
MoCA or MMSE Memory, attention, language, executive function Screens for early dementia/Alzheimer's; early intervention and medication work best when started early; reversible causes (B12, thyroid, depression) must be ruled out first Forgetfulness, getting lost in familiar places, difficulty managing money/medications, repeating questions MoCA more sensitive than MMSE for mild impairment. Administer in his language. 10-minute questionnaire; should be done annually in this age group.
Depression Screening (PHQ-9 or GDS-15) Mood and depressive symptoms Common and under-reported in Indian men; presents as pain/fatigue/irritability, not sadness; compounded by recent widowerhood Low energy, loss of interest in usual activities, sleep changes, withdrawal, hopelessness ⚠ Especially important — he recently lost his wife. Grief & depression overlap. Ask privately about suicidal thoughts. Worth flagging the recent loss to whoever does this.
Carotid Doppler Arterial blood flow to brain Stroke prevention screening Transient weakness, vision loss, slurred speech See Whole-Body Imaging above.

14. Eye, Ear & Dental

Test What It Assesses Why Essential at 70+ Symptoms / Red Flags if Abnormal Key Notes / Instructions
Comprehensive Eye Exam (Visual Acuity, IOP, Slit Lamp, Dilated Fundus) Vision, glaucoma, cataracts, diabetic retinopathy, macular degeneration Vision loss = major cause of falls & cognitive decline; glaucoma silent until late Blurred vision, halos around lights, difficulty reading, frequent falls, bumping into objects Pupil dilation is required — bring sunglasses and do not drive afterward. Include dilated fundoscopy (ICMR mandates annual in diabetics) + tonometry (glaucoma) + cataract assessment. AAO recommends every 1–2 years ≥65.
Tonometry (Intraocular Pressure) Pressure inside the eye Screens for glaucoma Peripheral vision loss (often unnoticed by patient until severe) Part of comprehensive eye exam.
Audiometry (Hearing Test) Hearing across frequencies Hearing loss leads to social isolation, depression, and accelerated cognitive decline Asking people to repeat, TV volume very high, missing phone calls/doorbells, social withdrawal, tinnitus Non-invasive; ~15–20 minutes. Pure-tone audiometry in soundproof booth (whisper tests insufficient). Correcting hearing = high-impact dementia-risk reduction. Check/remove cerumen first.
Dental Checkup + Panoramic X-Ray Oral health, gum disease, oral cancer Poor dental health is linked to heart disease and diabetes; oral cancer risk rises with age and tobacco use; poor dentition → malnutrition Bleeding gums, loose teeth, mouth sores, difficulty chewing, chronic bad breath Often neglected but critical. Combine with oral-cancer visual exam. Essential before bisphosphonate therapy for osteoporosis.

15. Infectious Disease & Immunity

Test What It Assesses Why Essential at 70+ Symptoms / Red Flags if Abnormal Key Notes / Instructions
HBsAg + Full Hepatitis B Panel (anti-HBs, anti-HBc) Active Hepatitis B infection and immunity India has intermediate prevalence; chronic infection leads to cirrhosis and liver cancer Fatigue, jaundice, abdominal pain, swelling If negative and not immune, discuss Hepatitis B vaccination. Complete HBV panel, not HBsAg alone.
Anti-HCV (with reflex HCV RNA if positive) Hepatitis C exposure Curable if detected early; often silent for decades before destroying the liver Usually asymptomatic for years If antibody positive, requires HCV RNA confirmatory test. WHO recommends one-time HCV screening for adults.
HIV 1 & 2 HIV infection Baseline screening; important if any history of blood transfusions Frequent infections, weight loss, night sweats, persistent diarrhea Confidential testing is standard at all major hospitals.
TB Screening (IGRA / Quantiferon preferred; symptom screen ± CXR ± sputum NAAT) Latent or active tuberculosis TB is endemic in India; reactivation risk increases with age and diabetes (diabetes doubles TB risk) Cough >3 weeks, weight loss, night sweats, low-grade fever IGRA blood test is preferred over skin test (Mantoux) because most Indians received BCG vaccine, which causes false-positive skin tests. Do NOT use IGRA/TST to screen for active TB — high clinical suspicion if cough >2 weeks, weight loss, fever.
H. pylori Testing (stool antigen or urea breath test) Gastric infection Very prevalent in India; causes B12 deficiency, dyspepsia, ulcers, gastric cancer risk Chronic acidity, bloating, ulcer history Consider given B12-deficiency link and any dyspepsia. Non-invasive.

16. Vaccination Review

Ask the physician to verify vaccination records and administer any missing doses. This is preventive medicine, not a "test," but it belongs on the same checklist.

Vaccine What It Prevents Why Essential at 70+ Key Notes
Pneumococcal (PCV20 or PCV15 + PPSV23) Pneumonia, meningitis, bloodstream infections Leading cause of hospitalization and death in elderly Indians Ask which schedule applies; often one dose of PCV20 is sufficient at this age.
Influenza (Annual) Seasonal flu Flu can be fatal in elderly; strains change yearly Take every year. A high-dose version is specifically made for adults 65+.
Tdap (Tetanus, Diphtheria, Pertussis) Tetanus and whooping cough Booster every 10 years; pertussis protection also protects grandchildren One Tdap in adulthood, then Td every 10 years.
Shingles (Shingrix) Shingles and post-herpetic neuralgia Risk rises dramatically with age; pain can be debilitating and last for months/years Two doses 2–6 months apart. Recommended even if he had shingles before.
Hepatitis B Liver infection/cancer If blood test shows no immunity Usually 2-dose series (Heplisav-B) or 3-dose depending on brand.
COVID-19 COVID-19 Elderly are highest-risk group for severe outcomes Keep up to date with current boosters as recommended.
RSV Respiratory syncytial virus Newly available for adults 60+; discuss with doctor Shared clinical decision-making based on risk.

These are not for everyone, but should be discussed with the doctor if there are specific risk factors, symptoms, or family history.

Test / Investigation When to Consider It What It Assesses
Stool Routine / Occult Blood Persistent bowel complaints, anemia, or as alternative to colonoscopy Parasites, infection, hidden blood
Rheumatoid Factor (RF) & Anti-CCP Joint pain, morning stiffness, swelling Rheumatoid arthritis
ANA (Antinuclear Antibody) Unexplained rashes, joint pain, sun sensitivity, fatigue Autoimmune disorders (lupus, etc.)
Spirometry / PFT History of smoking, chronic cough, breathlessness COPD, asthma, reduced lung function
Sleep Study (Polysomnography) or STOP-BANG Questionnaire Loud snoring, daytime sleepiness, morning headaches Sleep apnea — linked to hypertension and heart disease. If high suspicion, do polysomnography/home sleep study (questionnaires alone don't diagnose).
Holter Monitoring (24–48 hr) Palpitations, unexplained fainting, irregular heartbeat on ECG Intermittent arrhythmias
Monofilament Test Diabetes present Peripheral neuropathy (nerve damage in feet)

Serum Protein Electrophoresis (SPEP) Persistent bone pain (especially back), unexplained anemia, frequent infections, kidney issues, high calcium Multiple Myeloma and related disorders — peak incidence in the 70s. Essential if any unexplained anemia or bone pain.

18. Geriatric & Functional Assessments (Highest-Yield, Most Missing)

A Comprehensive Geriatric Assessment (CGA) predicts disability, hospitalization, and death better than any single lab. Request a geriatric-medicine consult if available.

Test / Assessment What It Assesses Why Essential at 70+ Symptoms / Red Flags if Abnormal Key Notes / Instructions
Blood Pressure — Orthostatic (lying + standing at 1 & 3 min) Drop in BP on standing Major reversible fall contributor; often medication-driven (alpha-blockers, diuretics, antihypertensives, sedatives) Lightheadedness, blackouts, falls Measure lying/seated, then standing at 1 & 3 minutes. Review medications that may be causing this.
BMI + Waist Circumference Obesity / metabolic risk (and low weight/sarcopenia) Asians have higher body-fat/cardiometabolic risk at lower BMI — use Asian cutoffs (overweight ≥23, obese ≥25 kg/m²) High: metabolic risk. Low/falling: frailty, cancer, TB Waist >90 cm (men) = central obesity (matters more than BMI in Indians). Track unintentional weight loss.
Fall Risk — Timed Up-and-Go (TUG) + Gait/Balance Fall/fracture risk Falls = leading cause of death and independence loss in elderly; often preventable Slow/unsteady gait, prior falls, fear of falling, holding furniture TUG >12–13 sec = high fall risk. Combine with vision, orthostatic BP, med review, home-hazard advice, vitamin D.
Frailty — Fried Phenotype / Clinical Frailty Scale + Grip Strength Frailty syndrome Predicts adverse outcomes; changes how aggressively to screen/treat everything else Weight loss, exhaustion, weakness (low grip), slow walking, low activity A frail phenotype argues against aggressive cancer screening & for focusing on function. This is the lens for all other results. EWGSOP2 cutoffs: grip
Nutrition — Mini Nutritional Assessment (MNA) Malnutrition / sarcopenia risk Widowed, possibly eating poorly alone; malnutrition drives frailty, poor immunity, falls Weight loss, low BMI, low albumin, poor intake Tie to serum albumin and weight trend. Ask for protein target 1.0–1.2 g/kg; consider whey if deficient.
Sarcopenia — Grip Strength (dynamometer), Gait Speed, Calf Circumference Muscle loss Indian vegetarian elderly diets are often protein-deficient; muscle loss drives frailty/falls Weakness, slow walking, difficulty rising from chairs, stairs EWGSOP2 cutoffs: grip
Polypharmacy / Medication Review Inappropriate or interacting medications Elderly accumulate prescriptions; adverse drug events = top hospitalization cause Dizziness, falls, confusion, bleeding, kidney injury Bring EVERY pill + OTC + herbal. Review against Beers Criteria. Deprescribing = as valuable as any new test.
Functional Status — ADLs / IADLs Independence in daily living Establishes baseline the son can monitor remotely from the U.S. Difficulty bathing, dressing, cooking, managing money/medications Simple questionnaire. Ask son to compare current vs. 1–2 years ago.
Sleep Apnea Screen — STOP-BANG Obstructive sleep apnea Snoring/witnessed apnea → resistant HTN, nocturia, cognition/mood decline, CV risk Snoring, witnessed apnea, daytime sleepiness, morning headaches If high suspicion, do polysomnography/home sleep study (questionnaires alone don't diagnose).
Urinary Incontinence Screen Urge/stress/overflow incontinence Often prostate/neurologic/medication-driven; causes falls rushing to toilet Leakage, nocturia, urgency Review diuretics, diabetes, constipation, prostate symptoms.
Home-Safety / Fall-Hazard Review Environmental risks Falls preventable with simple modifications Near-falls, unsafe bathroom/stairs Grab bars, night lighting, remove loose rugs, footwear check. Consider physiotherapy home assessment.
Advance Care Planning Goals of care, emergency plan Prevents crisis decisions later — Document emergency contacts, medication list, allergies, preferred hospital, insurance, decision-maker if incapacitated.

19. Specialist Consultations

Consultation / Test What It Checks Why It Matters Signs / Red Flags Notes for the Doctor
Geriatric Medicine / Internal Medicine Consult Orchestrates CGA, synthesizes everything The "quarterback" who prevents over-testing and integrates results into a life-expectancy-appropriate plan — Book this FIRST if possible — a geriatrician will right-size the entire battery.
Cardiology Consult Integrates BP/ECG/echo/lipid/CAC findings Synthesizes CV workup given age + South-Asian risk — Bring lipid, ApoB, Lp(a), hs-CRP, ECG, echo results.
Urology Consult Prostate (BPH/cancer), urinary symptoms Near-universal prostate issues at this age Urinary symptoms, high PSA If PSA elevated or urinary symptoms present. Complete IPSS before visit.
Comprehensive Eye Exam (Ophthalmology) Cataract, glaucoma, diabetic retinopathy, macular degeneration, refraction Vision loss = major cause of falls & cognitive decline; glaucoma silent until late Blurred vision, halos, field loss, difficulty reading Include dilated fundoscopy (diabetic retinopathy — ICMR mandates annual in diabetics) + tonometry (glaucoma) + cataract assessment. AAO recommends every 1–2 years ≥65.
Hearing Test (Audiometry / ENT) Age-related hearing loss (presbycusis) Strongly linked to social isolation, depression, accelerated dementia Asking to repeat, TV volume high, social withdrawal, tinnitus Pure-tone audiometry in soundproof booth (whisper tests insufficient). Correcting hearing = high-impact dementia-risk reduction. Check/remove cerumen first.
Dental Examination Oral health, gum disease, oral cancer, chewing ability Poor dentition → malnutrition; periodontal disease linked to CVD; oral cancer screen (tobacco burden) Tooth loss, gum disease, non-healing lesions, difficulty chewing Combine with oral-cancer visual exam. Essential before bisphosphonate therapy for osteoporosis.

20. Suggested 3-Week Sequencing

Week Activities
Week 1 Geriatric/internal-medicine consult (right-size everything) + one fasting blood draw (all panels: CBC, CMP, lipids + ApoB/Lp(a)/hs-CRP, HbA1c/FPG, B12-Active/MMA, vitamin D, iron, TSH, homocysteine, uric acid, urine + ACR). BP (both arms + orthostatic), ECG. Book specialist & imaging slots.
Week 2 Imaging: abdominal + pelvic USG (with aorta + post-void residual + prostate volume), DEXA hip+spine, LDCT chest only if smoking-eligible, AAA if ever-smoked. Eye (dilated + tonometry), audiometry, dental/oral-cancer exam. Geriatric assessments: MoCA, GDS-15, TUG, grip strength, MNA, ADL/IADL, STOP-BANG.
Week 3 Results review with physician/geriatrician. Shared-decision items: PSA (free/total ratio), FIT vs colonoscopy, stress test only if symptomatic. Referrals to cardiology/urology/GI as needed. Vaccination catch-up + medication rationalization (Beers Criteria) + fall-prevention + a remote monitoring plan the son can track from the U.S.

Two Sentences to Say to Every Doctor

"He's a mid-70s senior — please establish a baseline, screen appropriately for his life expectancy, and flag reversible problems."

"Please use age-adjusted ranges for TSH, PSA, and kidney function, and specifically order ApoB, Lp(a) once-in-a-lifetime, hs-CRP, Active B12, and cystatin C — they're not in the standard package."

06 · Often Missed

Tests Packages Miss

Five assessments most executive health-check packages leave out entirely.

1. Cognitive Screening

Recommended

MoCA (Montreal Cognitive Assessment), or Mini-Cog.

Checks

  • Memory
  • Attention
  • Executive function

Early cognitive decline can often be managed better when identified early.

2. Frailty Assessment

Ask for

Clinical Frailty Scale.

Looks at

  • Independence
  • Walking
  • Daily activities
  • Physical reserve

A healthy 75-year-old and a frail 75-year-old are medically very different.

3. Fall Risk Assessment

Should include

Timed Up and Go Test.

Checks

  • Mobility
  • Balance
  • Fall risk

4. Muscle Loss Assessment

Age-related muscle loss (sarcopenia) can increase falls, hospitalization risk, and loss of independence.

Useful

  • Hand grip strength
  • Body composition assessment

5. Nutrition Assessment

Ask

  • Is protein intake adequate?
  • Is weight changing unintentionally?
  • Is vitamin deficiency present?

07 · Questions

Questions to Ask Before Booking

Call the hospital and ask these four questions before paying for anything.

Question 1

"Is this package designed specifically for people above 70, or is it just an executive checkup?"

Question 2

"Does it include geriatric assessment?"

Ask specifically about

  • Cognition
  • Falls
  • Frailty
  • Nutrition

Question 3

"Who reviews abnormal results?"

Not just a PDF sent over — you need a physician interpretation.

Question 4

"Can we add targeted tests after initial results?"

Good hospitals should allow this.
